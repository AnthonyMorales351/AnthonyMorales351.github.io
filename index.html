Anthony Morales

Clinical Therapist📚

My journey toward becoming a therapist began long before I stepped into a university classroom. Growing up, I was always the person friends came to when they needed to vent. I didn’t realize then that I was practicing the foundations of active listening, holding space for someone else’s struggle without needing to "fix" it immediately. After graduating high school, I entered the workforce, eager to gain independence but still searching for a path that felt meaningful. At Lowe’s, I learned the value of patience and de-escalation, as dealing with frustrated customers looking for specific parts or navigating complex home projects taught me how to read people under pressure. I became the employee who could turn a tense interaction into a productive one simply by staying calm, validating their frustration, and offering a clear, supportive solution. My time at the movie theater offered a different kind of insight. Working in a high-traffic environment, I saw thousands of people pass through our doors.

I observed how people sought escapism, connection, and comfort in stories. I realized that everyone carries a narrative as a personal history that shapes how they interact with the world. Whether I was helping a family choose a film or managing a busy concession stand, I was constantly observing human dynamics. I learned how to connect with people from all walks of life, often in the span of a thirty-second transaction. These experiences were my "pre-clinical" training. They taught me that therapy isn't just about academic theory; it’s about meeting people where they are. Whether a customer was stressed about a renovation or a patron was looking for a moment of joy, I learned that being present and genuinely interested in their experience was the most valuable tool I had. Now, I am fully committed to formalizing that passion. I am currently pursuing my education with a singular focus, which is to become a licensed therapist.

I understand that this is a long road, requiring both a Master’s degree and a PhD but I am prepared for the task ahead. I view my academic journey as the necessary structure to support the intuition I’ve built over the years. I want to move beyond just listening to my friends or de-escalating customer conflicts; I want to understand the psychological mechanisms behind human suffering and resilience. I am drawn to this field because I believe in the power of the therapeutic relationship to change lives. I want to help individuals navigate their own narratives, just as I’ve learned to navigate the diverse personalities I encountered in my previous jobs. I am excited to trade the retail vest for the therapist’s chair. I know that the transition from the theater lobby to the counseling office is significant, but I am ready to do the work. I am driven by the belief that everyone deserves to be heard, and I am dedicated to gaining the clinical expertise necessary to provide that support at the highest level. This is more than a career change, as it is the realization of a purpose I’ve been cultivating for years.
